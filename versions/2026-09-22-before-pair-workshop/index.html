04 / BATTLE PLAN · BALANCING & LOGIC

Reward progress.
Respect the budget.

Make the rules and assumptions executable. Ask AI to examine boundaries, counterexamples, and scenarios—not just produce a reassuring number.

points / qualifying activityDaily cap: pointsEligibility: points + distinct daysReward limit: 1 / member / week

Model: 1,000 members/week × 60% eligible × redemption × unit cost × 4 weeks. Worst case: every member redeems once each week. Budget: $5,000. USD values are illustrative; no forecast, real rewards, or purchases.

What should the model challenge?

Repeated same-day logs hit the earning cap. One active day cannot satisfy a three-day rule. Activities below the minimum earn no points. New weeks start fresh.

Alignment check: one qualifying activity on each of 3 days earns 30 points. The 60-point reward threshold requires 6 rewarded activities. Is that extra demand right for a busy returner? Treat it as an open design decision.

AI’s role: write test cases, explore sensitivity and distributions, find inconsistent rules. We review assumptions and verify the calculations.

Economy · 4 min. Default expected cost is $1,920, but the upper-bound scenario is $8,000: expected affordability is not a guarantee. Move cost to $1.25: the full-participation bound fits $5,000 under this model. This is deterministic scenario math, not a statistical forecast. A production model needs observed behavior, cost variability, fraud/duplicate handling, and enforceable reward inventory. Tests accompany the repository logic.
